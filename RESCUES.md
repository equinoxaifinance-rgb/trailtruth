# Real rescues after an app guessed

Each case was reported by a rescue team, a sheriff, or a news outlet quoting them. Under each one is what TrailTruth does instead.

## Mount Shasta, California (reported Sept. 3, 2026 · Google Gemini)

Three hikers told the sheriff they relied heavily on Gemini for the route and what to pack. The sheriff said Gemini advised them to bring far less food and water than they needed. They reached the summit at 7 p.m., seven hours after the noon turnaround, wandered off route into Mud Creek Canyon in the dark, and spent the night out with a knee injury until Forest Service climbing rangers reached them.

**What TrailTruth does instead.** It never tells you how much food or water to carry. It shows the mapped water along your route and says plainly when a day has none. Walking times come from distance plus the climb in USGS elevation data, and it warns you when the walking runs longer than the daylight. It says when the sun sets where you are standing.

Source: [ABC7, Siskiyou County Sheriff](https://abc7news.com/story/3-hikers-say-used-ai-plan-trip-rescued-becoming-stranded-mount-shasta/19785375/)

## Tatra Mountains, Poland (July 4, 2026 · ChatGPT)

Two hikers asked ChatGPT for a shortcut to the Five Lakes Valley. It sent them over the Świnicka Ławka crossing, and they got stuck in climbing terrain on a north face. The Tatra rescue service (TOPR) flew them out by helicopter.

**What TrailTruth does instead.** It routes you along mapped trails only. Ask which way a place is and it gives the distance by trail, not just a straight line. If you are off trail, it tells you when the straight line back to the trail crosses a mapped cliff and says not to take it.

Source: [TOPR weekly report](https://topr.pl/en/pracowity-poczatek-lipca-dla-ratownikow-topr-2/)

## Crown Mountain, British Columbia (October 2026 · an AI-planned route)

A 16-year-old followed an AI route up Crown Mountain by a gully and got stuck at the base of a cliff. North Shore Rescue reached him. Their search manager said: “You can’t just blindly follow what AI is proposing to you.”

**What TrailTruth does instead.** It will not tell you a route is easy or safe for you. Ask how hard a hike is and it says the map data has no difficulty ratings and to ask a ranger, then gives you the facts it does have: the trail's length and how much it climbs.

Source: [MobileSyrup](https://mobilesyrup.com/2026/10/05/bc-teen-cliff-stranded-ai-route-plan/)

## Howe Sound Crest Trail, British Columbia (July 15, 2026 · Google Maps)

Google Maps told three teenagers the trip would take 5 hours and 13 minutes. The hike takes 8 to 14 hours and is often a two- or three-day trip. One of them fell and was airlifted out. Google said its walking directions are designed for streets.

**What TrailTruth does instead.** It is built for trails, not streets. Times count the climb (1 hour for every 600 m up, on top of 5 km/h), are labelled as a rule of thumb without breaks, and the planner warns when a day is longer than its daylight.

Source: [The Narwhal](https://thenarwhal.ca/bc-hiking-avoid-google-maps/)

## Mount Fromme, British Columbia (2023 · Google Maps)

North Shore Rescue said people kept getting lost on Mount Fromme because Google Maps showed a trail that does not exist. One hiker had to be lifted off a cliff by helicopter.

**What TrailTruth does instead.** Its trails come from the National Park Service and USGS. Paths that come from community mapping (OpenStreetMap) are labelled as not official park trails whenever a route uses them, so you know which lines to trust less.

Source: [The Narwhal](https://thenarwhal.ca/bc-hiking-avoid-google-maps/)

## Causey Pike, Lake District, England (January 2, 2023 · AllTrails)

A walker took a shortcut shown on AllTrails as it got dark and could not find the path. Keswick Mountain Rescue’s log says: “There is no path in this location, the mapping software was incorrect.” They guided him out by phone.

**What TrailTruth does instead.** The map, your position, the way out and the emergency screen all work with no signal, because the whole park is on your phone. The way out is the shortest mapped route back to a road or trailhead from where you stand.

Source: [Keswick MRT incident log](https://keswickmrt.org.uk/rescue/causey-pike-3/)

What TrailTruth can't do: it can't see snow, ice, storms or a washed-out bridge, and it doesn't know how fit you are. When you ask about those it says it doesn't know, and with signal it shows the National Weather Service alerts and the park's own alerts.
